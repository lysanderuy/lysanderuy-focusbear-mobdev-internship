# Onboarding Task - Working with Vulnerable Populations

## Research Questions

### Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?

1. Children and adolescents
2. Older adults
3. People with disabilities (physical, cognitive, sensory, mental health)
4. Low-income individuals or families
5. Ethnic or racial minorities
6. LGBTQ+ individuals
7. Migrants, refugees, and undocumented individuals
8. People with limited digital literacy
9. Individuals experiencing mental health challenges
10. Survivors of abuse or violence

These groups are considered the vulnerable populations and they may face challenges in the following:

1. Privacy & Data Exploitation
2. Cyberbullying & Harassment
3. Accessibility Barriers
4. Digital Literacy Gaps
5. Algorithmic Bias
6. Financial Exploitation
7. Limited Access (Digital Divide)

### What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals?

When designing or working with neurodivergent individuals, ethical considerations include being aware that these individuals may experience cognitive overload more easily which means we need to avoid overwhelming UX, having respect for sensory needs as some may be more sensitive to flashing elements or bright light, and also practicing clear and direct communication by labelling buttons and actions clearly.

### How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism?

1. Reduce cognitive overload
2. Keep interfaces clean and structured
3. Breaking tasks into smaller steps
4. Minimizing distractions
5. Provide predictability on layouts and navigation patterns
6. Respecting sensory needs
7. Allowing customization

### How can we support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting?

To support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting, we have to keep everything clear, predictable, and structured. We need to foster open communication and focus on creating an environment that values clarity, flexibility, and respect for different working styles.

To be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates, I need to think in their shoes. I need to keep my communication clear and direct.
