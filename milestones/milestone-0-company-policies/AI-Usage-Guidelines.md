# Onboarding Task - Use of AI Tools

## Research Questions

### What AI tools are typically used for your role?

For my role as a Mobile Development Intern, I usually use Cursor AI which uses advanced models like GPT-4 or Claude 3.5 when I am on Cursor. When working on VS Code, I make use of GitHub Copilot.

### What are the benefits and risks of using AI in a professional setting?

In a professional setting, making use of AI as a tool makes work more efficient and allows increase in productivity. But only when used correctly. AI allows us, as developers, to pin point or narrow down causes much quicker by feeding it error logs or stack traces. AI can also serve as a tool for documentation where it can be used to explain complex APIs or summarize long setup guides. Further, AI can even make the developers more creative and open-minded by giving more options or explorable solutions. As for its risks, I think one major risk of using AI is when the user fully depends on AI to accomplish tasks, and deliver outputs. AI should only be used as a tool and never as a replacement.

### What types of information should never be entered into AI tools?

Personal, sensitive or confidential information should never be fed into AI tools. We should always treat AI as if it can be publicly accessed which means data such as passwords, emails, secrets could be compromised.

### How can you fact-check and validate AI-generated content to ensure accuracy?

AI-generated content should always be cross-checked with trusted and authorized sites. We should always verify these types of content by comparing with factual data, specially when dealing with important information like medical statistics, or official documentation.

### What steps will you take to ensure data privacy when using AI tools?

1. I will never input or feed personal, sensitive, or confidential information to AI.
2. I will always anonymize data for testing or exploring ideas.
3. I will use sample data instead of real user data or system information when making prompts.
4. I will always treat AI as if it can be publicly accessed by anyone.

## Reflection

### When should you use AI for assistance, and when should you rely on your own skills?

AI should only be used for aiding in debugging, documentations, or exploring solutions. As the developers, we should always sit as the final say for AI-generated output and we should always verify and test thoroughly what AI gives us. When writing program logic, specific algorithms, or critical decisions, we should solely rely on ourselves and non purely base on AI suggestions.

### How can you avoid over-reliance on AI while still benefiting from it?

Making it a practice to only use AI as a second opinion that still needs verification and review allows us to always question what AI gives us. Making it a habit to outline everything that is needed first such as system architecture, structures, and data flow before asking AI to do everything allows us to judge the output of AI whether we can make use of it or no. You should always be in control and only use AI as a tool when needed.
