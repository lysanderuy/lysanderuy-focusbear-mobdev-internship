# Onboarding Task - Occupational Health & Safety (OHS) for Desk-Based Work

## Goal

Better understand how to set up a safe and ergonomic workspace, especially when using a laptop, and develop habits to
prevent strain and discomfort.

---

## Research Questions

### What are the risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard

- Using a laptop without these peripherals can lead to **Ergonomic and Health Risks**
  - where _Neck and Back Pain_ becomes a problem as the screen is below eye level forcing the user to look down which
    leads to slouching, upper back pain, and neck strain over time.
  - where _Wrist and Hand Strain_ becomes a problem as typing on the laptop keyboard and using its trackpad requires
    awkward wrist positions which increases the risk of repetitive strain injuries like **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome**.
- It also raises **Usability and Performance Risks**
  - where _Productivity Reduces_ too as the awkward positions required to effectively use the laptop keyboard makes
    typing slower and less accurate, especially during long hours.
  - using the laptop screen alone _Limits Multitasking_ because it blocks the ability to view or navigate on multiple
    tabs and windows at once, which reduces efficiency.

### What ergonomic equipment can improve posture when working on a laptop

- **Laptop Stand** - Raising your laptop to be eye level can prevent bending the neck for a prolonged period which
  helps relieve neck pain and poor posture.
- **External Monitor** - Using a separate not only allows multitasking and improves productivity but it also helps
  relieve the needed tilt for the neck as everything, placed at a distance, is eye level.
- **External Keyboard** - Investing in an external keyboard allows you to avoid the awkward wrist positions needed
  to operate a laptop keyboard and allows you to be more efficient in your work as typing on an external keyboard
  is a lot easier.
- **Keyboard Wrist Pad** - Supporting the wrist from floating while typing significantly improves wrist pain and
  allows you to position your hand strategically to type more efficiently.
- **Mouse** - Using a mouse allows you to place your arm in a more relax position at the side of screen. It makes
  navigating windows and tabs more efficient.
- **Ergonomic Chairs** - With an ergonomic chair, your entire back and neck is supported in a natural angle which
  becomes useful for back and neck pain relief especially on long shifts.
- **Lumbar and Neck Supports** - Optionally, on a non-ergonomic chair, you may place a lumbar and neck pillow to
  support the natural curve and structure of your back to make working more comfortable.

### What adjustments should be made to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup for a healthier workspace

- **Monitor Height** - You should position the monitor to be slight below eye level and slightly inclined towards the
  eyes to improve visibility. It should also be place around an arms length away on the desk.
- **Chair Position** - You should adjust the seat height to where your feet naturally rest flat on the ground and
  thighs parallel to the floor. You should seat fully back into the chair to ensure that the lumbar and neck support
  fits or follows the natural curve of the back. Also adjust arm rests to make the elbows rest at around 100° with
  shoulders relaxed.
- **Desk Setup** - Desk height should allow the elbows to be 90° when typing, with wrists straight and forearms
  parallel to the floor. Your desk should also have adequate space underneath the table for legroom.

### What are some daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use

- It is generally good to take a **short break** every now and then to relax the body. It is also recommended
  to **take a look at something 20ft. away to relax the eye muscles**. Every hour, **get up and walk around for
  5 minutes** to improve circulation and reduce stiffness. You should also incorporate some **stretches and exercises**
  during these breaks.

## Reflection

### What equipment changes can you make to improve your workspace setup

- I usually work anywhere I feel like in the house. A major change would be to **establish a single workspace** and
  desk setup to use. I also usually only work the laptop alone with no peripherals. I would **invest in a laptop stand,
  and an external keyboard** to allow for better posture and reduce musculoskeletal complications and eye strain over
  time. **Making use of an ergonomic chair** or even an simple office chair with **adjustable height** would also
  significantly improve posture as I work on my tasks and deliverables.

### What behavioural changes can you implement to improve posture and reduce strain

- One would be to **ensure that my desk setup is correct and everything is positioned correctly** every start of my day
  to avoid any risks of pain after working for long periods daily. I would also make use of the **built-in reminders for
  movement breaks** on the Focus Bear app to improve circulation, and allow the body to relax a little after just
  sitting for a number of hours.

### How can you remind yourself to maintain good posture and take breaks throughout the day

- For posture, one very useful tip a friend of mine once said was to always **think high or that someone is pulling
  you from above.** This forces me to sit upright every time I feel my body starting to want to slouch again.
